01
Context

The Real Problem

IBM's Financial Services Market teams were full of brilliant, technically deep account teams — but brilliant and consistent aren't the same thing. Opportunity discovery was hit-or-miss. Strategic initiatives lived in scattered docs, tribal knowledge, and varying formats. There was no shared picture of what was happening across accounts, let alone why.

The result? Great work happening in silos, with limited visibility for leadership and no repeatable way to connect client priorities to IBM opportunities at scale.

What I set out to do

Build a planning framework so good that teams would want to use it — one that aligned opportunities to client priorities, surfaced what was blocking progress, created clear ownership, and generated real pipeline. Consistently. At scale. Without adding a ton of overhead.

Who Was in the Room

This wasn't a small lift. Pulling this off meant coordinating across seven distinct stakeholder groups — all with different priorities, workflows, and definitions of "done."

Account Technical Leaders

The heart of it. They owned the accounts and drove the strategy in every session.

Client Engineering + Expert Labs

The builders. Brought technical depth and delivery credibility to every opportunity discussion.

BTS + CSMs

The connectors. Kept product and customer realities grounded in every conversation.

Red Hat + HashiCorp

The partners. Wove multi-product narratives into account stories that were bigger than IBM alone.

The Constraints (They Were Real)

Year 2

03
Strategic Framing

The Shift That Changed Everything

Most account planning starts with products. What can we sell? What's in the pipeline? I flipped that entirely.

The big idea

"Work Outside-In." Start every conversation with what the client is trying to accomplish — their strategy, their priorities, their definition of success. IBM's role comes second. Always.

That one reframe changed the whole dynamic. Instead of "Here's what IBM can offer," the conversation became "Here's what matters most to you — and here's exactly how we fit." Teams showed up differently. Clients engaged differently. Opportunities got better.

What I Was Building Toward

I wasn't just designing sessions — I was designing a system with four specific outcomes that I held myself accountable to from day one:

Clean Initiative Data

Every account leaving with accurate, complete Strategic Initiative records. No more guessing what clients actually care about.

Linked Opportunities

Every opportunity tied directly to a client initiative — so IBM could show up with relevance, not just presence.

Unblocked Pipeline

A clear picture of what's stuck and why — so teams could act, not speculate.

Market-Level Intelligence

Patterns across 63 accounts that no single team could see on their own — but leadership absolutely needed.

04
My Process
Phase 1

Design the Workshop

I built a Mural-based workshop experience from scratch — structured enough to produce consistent outputs, flexible enough that every account felt like it was built for them. It covered client initiatives, opportunity gaps, deployment ideas, blockers, and who owns what next. Simple on the surface. Powerful underneath.

Phase 2

Get Everyone Aligned Before the Room

Great sessions don't happen by accident. I worked with ATLs ahead of each session to define what they wanted to walk away with, who needed to be in the room, and what success looked like for their specific account. Prep made all the difference.

Phase 3

Scale Without Losing Quality

I couldn't personally run 63 sessions — so I built a facilitation system that could. I onboarded 14 strategy designers, created guides they could actually use, and put governance in place to keep quality consistent across every account. The goal was to make facilitators great, not just capable.

Phase 4

Turn 63 Accounts Into One Big Picture

After sessions wrapped, I built a tagging strategy across all opportunity data and mapped 60+ client initiatives into 8 macro themes. Suddenly, patterns that were invisible at the account level became clear signals at the market level — and leadership had intelligence they'd never had before.

Phase 5

Keep Getting Better

I ran retrospectives after every cycle — with facilitators, account teams, and leadership. Not as a formality, but because I genuinely wanted to know what wasn't working. Every cycle, the model got sharper. That's the point.
