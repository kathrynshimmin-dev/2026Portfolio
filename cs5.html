Context
Where This Came From

This work grew directly out of a bigger vision provocation: "What if digital commerce becomes the preferred purchasing channel, resulting in consumers expecting anytime, anywhere shopping experiences, forcing us to reimagine how our brands engage, convert, and serve customers online?" The pop-up was one answer to that question — a physical proof point that built brand conviction and drove the online experience conversation forward. See the full vision framework →

The Business Problem

Retail sales were down. Millennials — the growth audience Newell Brands needed — were shopping in ways the existing home fragrance experience wasn't built for. They valued experiences over things. They expected brands to earn their attention, not just occupy shelf space. A traditional candle display wasn't going to cut it anymore.

Problem Statement

Millennials need a more modernized home fragrance shopping experience because they value experiences over things — and retail sales are declining.

02
My Role

Workshop Participant & Designer

I contributed as both a workshop participant in the ideation process and a designer on the experience. That dual seat — being in the room where the concept was shaped and then helping bring it to life — gave me a complete view of how good design decisions get made when creative and strategic minds share the same whiteboard.

My focus was on the experience design: how the space communicated, how it felt to move through it, and how the brand story translated into something a visitor would stop, engage with, and share.

03
Strategic Framing

The Reframe

The instinct was to sell fragrance better. We reframed it: what if we stopped selling and started transporting? Scent is one of the most powerful memory-making senses humans have. Instead of describing fragrance, we designed an environment that let people experience what a scent could make them feel — then connected that feeling back to the brand.

The Strategic Bet

An Instagrammable NYC pop-up to build awareness of Newell Brands' home fragrance portfolio, drive trial, and engage new consumers — meeting Millennials where they live: in experiences worth sharing.

What Success Looked Like

Brand Awareness

Put the home fragrance portfolio in front of a Millennial audience that hadn't been actively seeking it.

Trial & Discovery

Get people to engage with fragrance in a way that felt personal, not transactional.

Social Amplification

Design spaces worth photographing — so the experience extended far beyond the four walls.

Earned Media

Create something compelling enough that press and trend forecasters would cover it organically.

Phase 2 — Ideation: The 5E Framework

We structured our ideation around the 5E Framework — a journey model that considers every phase of the consumer experience, from the moment they discover the pop-up to long after they've left it. This discipline kept us from designing a room and reminded us we were designing an arc.

01
Entice

How do we get Millennials to notice, stop, and want in?

02
Enter

What's the first moment? How does the brand declare itself?

03
Engage

What do people do? How do they interact with fragrance?

04
Exit

How do they leave? What do they take with them — physically and emotionally?

05
Extend

How does the experience live on — in posts, in memory, in future purchases?
