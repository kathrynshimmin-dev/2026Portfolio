Context

The Challenge

Many nonprofit organizations faced growing pressure to leverage data and AI but lacked the expertise, resources, and confidence to begin their adoption journey. They understood the potential — but not the path.

The Dual Opportunity

IBM needed to expand AI adoption into underserved sectors. Nonprofits needed a structured path to translate mission challenges into AI opportunities. The goal was to design a model that created value for both sides simultaneously.

Organizational Landscape

Nonprofit Leaders

Mission-driven executives seeking practical AI applications for their organizations.

Funders & Conveners

data.org, Echoing Green, and ecosystem partners shaping adoption pathways.

IBM Business Units

5 IBM organizations contributing technical, design, and strategic resources.

Executive Sponsors

Internal champions providing visibility and expansion support.

Key Constraints

  • Nonprofits with limited AI readiness and technical capacity
  • Need to create IBM business value alongside social impact
  • Multi-stakeholder coordination across IBM organizations and external partners
  • Program needed to prove itself before resources were committed to scale

03
Strategic Framing

A Two-Year Innovation Flywheel

Year 1 — Prove the Model

Validate whether a structured AI innovation framework could create value for both nonprofits and IBM. Success defined by pipeline creation, opportunity identification, partnership formation, and repeatability.

Year 2 — Expand and Mature

Building on pilot success, evolve the framework to engage additional organizations, deepen collaboration, and strengthen the ecosystem. Shift from proving value to creating a sustainable, scalable motion.

Success Criteria

Pipeline Creation

Generate qualified IBM opportunities from nonprofit engagements.

Use Case Discovery

Surface specific, implementable AI applications for each organization.

Partnership Formation

Establish ecosystem relationships that amplify reach and credibility.

Repeatability

Create a model that could be adopted by other IBM teams and markets.

04
My Process
Design Approach

I treated this as an ecosystem design problem — not a workshop execution problem. The goal was to build a system that could bring together nonprofits, executives, technologists, funders, and designers around a shared goal, and improve with each iteration.

Iteration 1

Pilot & Learn — Year 1

Tested workshop structure, refined facilitation methods, understood nonprofit AI readiness, and measured business outcomes. Key learnings:

  • Organizations engaged most when discussions centered on mission outcomes, not technology
  • Executive sponsors wanted clearer business measures
  • Funders wanted visibility into implementation readiness
  • Participants needed a structured path from ideas to actionable MVPs
Iteration 2

Refine & Scale — Year 2

Applied Year 1 learnings: strengthened opportunity prioritization, improved solution-scoping activities, increased executive engagement, expanded partnership participation, and created a more repeatable facilitation framework.

Design Director Reflection

The greatest success wasn't the workshop. It was the system behind it. By treating this as an ecosystem design problem, I created a repeatable model that brought together nonprofits, executives, technologists, funders, and designers around a shared goal. The first year proved the concept. The second year refined and expanded it.
